3D Renderer

Overview

This project was created to develop a deeper understanding of how 3D graphics are produced from mathematical representations of objects. Rather than relying on an existing game engine or high-level 3D rendering framework, I implemented the core rendering process myself.

The project takes 3D geometry, applies transformations and camera calculations, and converts it into a 2D representation that can be displayed on screen.

Key focus: understanding the mathematics and underlying implementation of a 3D graphics pipeline rather than simply using an existing rendering engine.

3D Transformations

The renderer uses mathematical transformations to manipulate objects within 3D space. Objects can be translated, rotated and scaled before being transformed into the camera's coordinate system.

This involved working with vectors and matrices to represent the position and orientation of objects and apply transformations efficiently.

Projection

A major part of the project was converting 3D coordinates into positions on a 2D screen. Perspective projection is used so that objects appear smaller as their distance from the camera increases.

Implementing this process helped me understand how concepts such as field of view, camera position and depth affect the final image produced by a renderer.

Rendering Pipeline

The renderer follows the fundamental stages required to turn geometric data into a visible image. Geometry is transformed through the 3D scene, projected onto the screen and then rendered as part of the final image.

Building these stages manually provided practical experience with the relationship between mathematical models, coordinate systems and graphical output.

What I Learned

  • Applying vector and matrix mathematics to a practical engineering problem
  • Working with 3D coordinate systems and transformations
  • Understanding perspective projection and camera geometry
  • Structuring a larger C# application across multiple classes and source files
  • Understanding the fundamentals of software-based 3D rendering
  • Debugging mathematical and graphical problems where errors are often not immediately visible
