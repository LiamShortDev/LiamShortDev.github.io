Tools used to create the LEGO assembly

Two different categories of CAD tools were used during the modelling process: Constrain and Joint, each serving a distinct purpose within the application.

The Constrain tool was used to define the fixed geometry of the model. Initially, the base component was fully constrained along the X, Y, and Z axes to remove all degrees of freedom and establish a stable reference frame. Subsequent components, such as individual bricks, were positioned by constraining two perpendicular edges. This method ensured that all translational and rotational movement was restricted, allowing precise and repeatable placement while maintaining dimensional accuracy across the model.

The Joint tool was used for components that required controlled motion. Rotational joints were applied to the steering wheel and wheel rims to replicate realistic circular motion about a single axis. This allowed the movement of these parts to be tested within the assembly, verifying that clearances and alignment were correct. The use of joints was particularly relevant for validating functional behaviour rather than just static geometry.
